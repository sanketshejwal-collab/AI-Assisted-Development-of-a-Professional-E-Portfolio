Abstract

This capstone project provides a comprehensive overview and diagnostic assessment of Lakaki Lake, located in Model Colony, Shivaji Nagar, Pune. Operating as an urban quarry lake surrounded by intense residential and commercial infrastructure, Lakaki Lake is subjected to heavy anthropogenic pressures that compromise its water quality, ecological health, and aquatic biodiversity. The main objective of this study is to evaluate the spatial and multi-decadal changes occurring in the lake between 2016, 2021, and 2026, specifically focusing on the anthropogenic pressure around the area and how it’s affecting the organisms and the natural habitat.

The project integrates primary field data collection using Epicollect5 mobile GIS, photographic logging of affected flora and fauna, and multi-spectral satellite remote sensing processing. Using ArcGIS and QGIS platforms, multi-spectral indices—including the Normalized Difference Built-Up Index (NDBI), Normalized Difference Red Edge Index (NDRE), Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI), and Normalized Difference Water Index (NDWI)—were generated across a 10-year timeline (2016, 2021, 2026). Rapid urban consolidation surrounding Model Colony has altered the natural hydrology and ecological equilibrium of the lake through four major pollution vectors: Spread and increment of Invasive Water Lettuce (Pistia stratiotes): Elevated nutrient concentration (eutrophication) combined with slow hydraulic turnover has created optimal conditions for invasive alien macrophytes. In addition to Water Hyacinth (Eichhornia crassipes), the lake is severely infested with Water Lettuce (Pistia stratiotes). Water Lettuce forms thick, interconnected floating mats across shallow shoreline bays, blocking light penetration, reducing photosynthetically active radiation for submerged flora, and driving severe dissolved oxygen depletion (DO < 2.0 mg/L). The anthropogenic pressure: As Lakaki lake is surrounded by the urban buildups, it faces a severe ecological degradation due to anthropogenic pressure, including polluted run offs, heavy human activities change throughout this time span, these changes affect the water quality, vegetation index which indirectly or directly affect the biodiversity of the areas. Human dumping of wastes, and feeding the organisms: We saw wastes such as the flower necklaces, leaves, plastic bottles etc., in the water and we also saw the residents living nearby feeding the organisms of the lake, its restricted but still going on illegally, which in turn pollutes the water and makes it difficult for the organisms.
